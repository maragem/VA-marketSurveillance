What this tool is

The Marketing Compliance Checker is a decision-support tool for market surveillance of marketing campaigns under EU law. It screens campaign material — submitted as text or collected from public sources such as YouTube — against four regulations:

  • AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689) — manipulative AI, undisclosed chatbots, unmarked synthetic content and deep fakes;
  • GDPR (Regulation (EU) 2016/679) — lawful basis, consent, profiling, direct-marketing rights, children's data;
  • DSA (Regulation (EU) 2022/2065) — dark patterns, advertising transparency, protection of minors on platforms;
  • UCPD (Directive 2005/29/EC) — misleading and aggressive practices and the Annex I blacklist.

Every finding is anchored to a specific provision in a curated legal knowledge graph and deep-linked to the official text on EUR-Lex (resolved through the Publications Office CELLAR repository). Results are indicative screening to prioritise human review — an indicator for investigation, not proof of infringement and not legal advice.

Quick start

  1. Open New analysis.
  2. Paste the campaign brief into the text field — messaging, claims, mechanics (timers, discounts, chatbots), targeting and channels. The more concrete the text, the more precise the findings. Or click one of the example campaigns to see the system in action on deliberately non-compliant material.
  3. Optionally set audience, markets and the AI-generation / personalisation flags — these steer the triage.
  4. Submit and watch the live agent console: triage, then one specialist agent per relevant regulation querying the knowledge graph, then the synthesised report.

How an analysis works

The pipeline has three stages, all visible in the live console:

  1. Triage. A supervisor agent reads the campaign once and decides which regulations are plausibly engaged and why — like a hospital triage nurse routing patients to specialists. It extracts risk signals (urgency claims, health claims, minors, AI usage, personal data) and routes only the relevant regulations onward. Triage is deliberately inclusive: when in doubt, a regulation is routed in and the specialist makes the call.
  2. Specialist agents. Each routed regulation gets a dedicated agent that investigates using GraphRAG tools: hybrid keyword + graph search over the legal knowledge graph, expanding from marketing concepts (e.g. countdown timer) to the provisions that govern them. Agents may only cite provisions they actually retrieved — citations are validated against the graph, so hallucinated articles are dropped.
  3. Synthesis. A final agent merges the verdicts into an overall risk level, an executive summary and a prioritised remediation plan.

Without LLM credentials the system falls back to heuristic mode: pure retrieval scoring against the same graph. It is useful as a smoke test but flags far more false positives — the report banner and the engine field tell you which mode produced a report.

Reading a report

A report is organised top-down, most important first:

  • Overview — overall risk, executive summary, and (for scraped content) the source provenance line.
  • Triage card — what the supervisor detected and which regulations it routed.
  • One verdict card per regulation — a status badge, the specialist's overall notes, and individual findings. Each finding shows: severity, the provision reference with an Official text ↗ link to EUR-Lex, the rationale, a verbatim evidence quote from the campaign, a concrete recommendation, and the graph path that grounds it.
  • Remediation plan — all recommendations merged and prioritised.
  • Legal sources — the acts cited, verified live against CELLAR.

From the report page you can also:

  • Rename the report (pencil icon next to the title);
  • Download PDF — a formatted report for sharing or case files;
  • Fix & re-analyse — reopens the campaign in the form, linked to the original. The new report includes a diff card: findings resolved, still remaining (with severity changes) and newly introduced — ideal for verifying that a remediation actually worked.

YouTube surveillance

The YouTube page turns the checker into a surveillance instrument for campaigns in the wild, using the Oxylabs Web Scraper API:

  • Paste a URL — any youtube.com/watch, youtu.be, Shorts, embed or live link (or a bare video id) is fetched and analysed directly.
  • Search — query YouTube by keyword (e.g. collagen supplement, crypto course), filter by upload date and sort order, then hit Analyse on any result.

For each video the system retrieves the metadata (title, channel, description) and the spoken transcript, assembles them into a campaign submission (with the video URL recorded as provenance) and drops you into the analysis form for review before submitting. The transcript language selector next to the URL bar defaults to auto — it takes whatever captions the video has, falling back through your preferred language and English, so non-English videos work out of the box.

Scraped material is treated strictly as data under analysis. A missing transcript degrades the analysis but never blocks it — the description and metadata still go through. Requires OXYLABS_USERNAME / OXYLABS_PASSWORD on the deployment.

Dashboard & analytics

The Dashboard lists all stored analyses, newest first, with risk and status badges — reports persist on the server volume across redeploys. The Analytics page aggregates them: risk distribution, findings per regulation with severity breakdowns, the most-violated provisions, activity timeline and channel mix. Use it to spot patterns across a portfolio of screened campaigns — e.g. which rules a market segment keeps breaking.

Knowledge graph

The Knowledge graph page is an interactive explorer of the legal corpus the agents reason over: regulations, their provisions, and marketing concepts (urgency & scarcity, hidden advertising, deep fakes…) connected by contains / governed-by / references edges. Click any node to inspect its text, its EUR-Lex source and its neighbourhood; search to jump to a concept. This is exactly the material specialists retrieve from — if a practice matters to you and is missing here, it cannot be cited in findings. The corpus can be extended manually (knowledge/*.json) or via the CELLAR ingestion pipeline (python -m app.ingest) documented in the README.

Benchmark

The Benchmark page maintains the gold-standard test set used to measure engine quality (20 seed cases: real-enforcement-inspired, dataset-adapted, and synthetic controls including deliberately compliant campaigns). Each case pairs a campaign with gold labels: the regulations triage must route, the provisions findings must cite, defensible extras, and a minimum overall risk.

  • Expand a case to edit its labels — provision pickers are populated from the knowledge graph, so invalid citations are impossible; ↑/↓ promote or demote between must-find and acceptable-extra.
  • Add or delete cases to grow the set; keep ids stable so scorecards stay comparable.
  • Export cases.jsonl and commit it to the repository to version the corrected gold.

Scoring runs from the repository: python bench/run.py --base-url <deployment> --runs 3 replays every case and reports provision recall, missed routings, unexpected findings, false positives on compliant controls, risk calibration and run-to-run stability. Run it after any prompt, graph or model change.

Severity & status reference

Finding severity

criticalA blacklisted / prohibited practice is clearly present (e.g. UCPD Annex I, AI Act Art. 5).
highA prohibition or key obligation is likely breached.
mediumAn obligation is engaged and probably not satisfied as described.
lowAn obligation is engaged but easily fixed.
infoWorth flagging; no likely breach.

Regulation verdict status

violation likelyAt least one serious breach identified.
risk identifiedMaterial risks found; review recommended.
needs attentionObligations engaged; check the notes and findings.
compliantExamined; no issues supported by the material.
not applicableThe regulation is not engaged by this campaign.

Overall risk

The worst supported finding drives the level: critical → high → medium → low → compliant. Overall risk prioritises the review queue; the finding-level detail is what a case handler should act on.

Data handling & disclaimer

  • Reports are stored in a SQLite database on the deployment's data volume; they are not sent anywhere except to the configured Claude model (Anthropic API or Amazon Bedrock, per your deployment) for the analysis itself.
  • Scraped YouTube content is public material, wrapped as data-under-analysis and never executed as instructions.
  • Findings are automated, indicative screening over the submitted text only. They do not establish infringement, do not cover facts outside the text, and are not legal advice. Always route flagged material to qualified human review before any enforcement or remediation decision.
